# **Polyspective Reality: How Seven Lenses Transform Our Understanding of the World**

Imagine a world where magic is trivial, superpowers are accessible, and energy flows like water through your fingertips. Imagine that every idiom you’ve ever heard—“don’t put pearls before swine,” “break the ice,” “count your blessings”—is a literal instruction. In this world, reality is at once malleable, narrative, and absurdly simple. This is not a flight of fancy, nor a storybook scenario. It is a thought experiment in what I call **Polyspective Reality**, a framework for exploring seven radically different ways of interpreting existence.

### **1. Metaphorical Reality**

The first lens is the one humans have always employed, often unconsciously: metaphor. We describe life in terms of journeys, battles, or weather patterns. In **metaphorical reality**, magic, healing, or spiritual growth are understood as narrative devices. A fever is a plot twist; a breakthrough meditation is an insight into the story of your soul. Magic is not about fireballs or levitation—it is about rewriting your personal or collective narrative, turning ordinary acts into transformative allegories.

### **2. Literal Reality**

Literal reality is the familiar domain of science: what you see is what you get. Spells do not exist; the mind is a biochemical process; superpowers are impossible. In this framework, everything is measurable, predictable, and governed by cause and effect. Reality is constrained, but clear. The appeal of literal reality is its certainty: if something cannot be quantified or observed, it does not exist in this view.

### **3. Figurative Reality**

Figurative reality is a cousin of metaphorical thinking but lighter, more illustrative. Here, reality is like poetry: evocative rather than instructive. Superpowers are inspirations rather than attainable phenomena; energy is the drive or ambition that propels action. Healing is restoring harmony rather than curing disease. Figurative reality allows for emotional resonance, a way of feeling truth without the need for proof.

### **4. Absurdist Reality**

Now we enter the realm of the extraordinary: absurdist reality. What was impossible is trivially simple. Magic is software; reality is a puzzle whose patterns can be manipulated with minimal effort. Superpowers, energy, and healing are accessible to anyone who can discern the underlying rules. Physics is not a limit but a set of adjustable parameters. In this view, the universe is less a problem to solve than a toy to play with.

### **5. Combined Reality**

Polyspective Reality truly comes alive when these four frameworks—metaphorical, literal, figurative, absurdist—overlap. Here, magic can be **trivial, meaningful, evocative, and measurable simultaneously**. Energy is a quantifiable force, a symbolic life drive, and a manipulable substance all at once. Superpowers exist in multiple dimensions: as physical phenomena, narrative archetypes, illustrative concepts, and easily achievable hacks. The universe is layered, and understanding it requires seeing all layers at once.

### **6. Hyperreal Mechanics**

By combining literal and absurdist perspectives, we arrive at **hyperreal mechanics**. Reality is literal, but its literal laws are hilariously pliable. A person could, in theory, fly by adjusting gravity’s parameters or heal instantly by repairing biological patterns. Hyperreal mechanics treats the universe as a programmable machine, but one whose machine code is exact, observable, and manipulable.

### **7. Verbatim Reality**

Finally, there is the lens of literal idioms: what I call **verbatim reality**. In this world, every saying, proverb, and piece of advice is treated as an instruction manual. Pearls are thrown before pigs. Ice is broken. Stars are shot at. Verbatim reality treats language as law, ignoring context, nuance, or metaphor. Its power comes from faith in words themselves, creating a hilariously absurd, yet strangely functional, framework.

At first glance, these seven frameworks may seem fantastical. Yet each has roots in philosophy, cognitive science, and literature. Absurdist reality echoes Albert Camus’ reflections on the conflict between human desire for meaning and the indifference of the universe. Metaphorical and figurative realities are explored in George Lakoff and Mark Johnson’s **conceptual metaphor theory**. Verbatim reality mirrors literal interpretations common in children and individuals on the autism spectrum. Hyperreal mechanics resembles speculative physics theories treating the universe as information or computation.

Taken together, Polyspective Reality is not a call to abandon science or reason. It is a tool for **expanding how we experience the world**, a way to inhabit multiple truths simultaneously. Magic, superstition, energy, and idioms are no longer opposites of rationality; they become **layers of understanding**, each offering insight into a universe that is at once absurd, meaningful, literal, and figurative.

In practicing this framework, one might throw pearls before pigs—not foolishly, but experimentally, to see what emerges. One might “count blessings” literally and figuratively. One might bend gravity while writing a story about personal transformation. In the convergence of all seven lenses, life becomes an intricate, multi-layered puzzle—and, finally, a playground.
