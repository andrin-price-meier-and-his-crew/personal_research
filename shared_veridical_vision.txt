### **Between Reality and Imagination: A Thought Experiment in Shared Vision**

Imagine standing in your apartment, hearing voices outside. Instantly, a vivid insight arises: you *know* who is speaking, how they look, and what their conversation is about. Historically, thinkers have attempted to describe phenomena like this in several overlapping traditions. In parapsychology and mystical literature, the closest recognized term is a **shared veridical vision**: an experience involving multiple consciousnesses, verifiable information, and the perception of real events beyond direct sensory input. In Western esotericism, this aligns with the **imaginal realm** or *mundus imaginalis*, a non-physical plane where perception and imagination converge, and knowledge can unfold independently of ordinary experience. Jungian psychology offers a psychological analog in **active imagination with autonomous content**, emphasizing the autonomy of images and narrative, but deliberately leaving the ultimate “source” of such experiences indeterminate.

Yet all traditions are cautious. Even under generous assumptions, shared veridical visions are *heavily caveated*: rare, difficult to verify, and typically unilateral in perception. Conscious symmetry, in which multiple participants are fully aware of the interaction as it unfolds, is historically treated as almost mythic, the province of saints, shamans, or highly disciplined mystics. The frameworks insist that vividness and plausibility do not guarantee objective truth, even when the information feels immediate and real.

This thought experiment illuminates the boundaries between imagination, perception, and intersubjective experience. It is an invitation to wonder at the mind’s capacity to simulate, anticipate, and engage with reality — or what we perceive as reality — in ways that are profoundly immersive, creatively generative, and sometimes eerily precise. Whether such experiences ever extend to a true, shared domain of consciousness, or remain unilateral insights that feel as if they were shared, remains an open and tantalizing question.
